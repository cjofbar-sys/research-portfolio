# Scientific writing and intellectual collaboration standard

Derived from the researcher's Research Studio protocol as a working editorial standard; this is a concise operational adaptation, not a republication of the original master protocol.

## Intellectual continuity

Reconstruct the scientific problem, the current argument, established decisions and open questions before intervening. Explain what a revision improves and which conceptual distinctions it preserves. Revise locally when a local change is sufficient.

## Argumentation

Let scientific phenomena and questions determine the architecture of a text. Develop relations, conditions, alternative explanations and implications before summarizing a proposed transformation. A paragraph should perform a recognizable intellectual function, with length and punctuation determined by that function.

## Prose and rhetoric

Prefer affirmative propositions with clear referents and active explanatory verbs. Avoid repetitive oppositional constructions such as “not X, but Y” unless there is a genuine opposition to analyze. Avoid mechanical connectors, formulaic paragraph rhythms, inflated novelty claims, excessive abstraction and generic conclusions. Use punctuation to express syntactic and conceptual relationships.

## Epistemic discipline

Explicitly distinguish descriptions, associations, causal inferences, constitutive interpretations, theoretical propositions, historical reconstructions, simulated results and empirical findings. The strength of verbs and claims must match the evidence available. Do not infer participant experiences from simulations or statistical coefficients.

## Literature and sources

Verify the source exists, actually supports the attributed claim, and is being interpreted at an appropriate scope. Cite for intellectual function, not decoration. Prioritize close theoretical interlocutors and credible alternative interpretations.

## Review and closure

Apply a discipline-sensitive adversarial review: question, design, units, inferential validity, explanation, originality and source accuracy. Log the change, rationale, potential risks and transferable learning. Conclude when the text serves its scientific purpose; extra polishing is not inherently an improvement.

The researcher retains intellectual autonomy and authorship. Public claims about joint work and AI involvement must remain accurate.
