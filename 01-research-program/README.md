# Research programme | Working public overview

This research programme examines how people construct, negotiate, and reconsider possibilities for action and becoming through historically situated sociocultural relations, imaginative activity, and mediated environments.

Its intellectual foundations include cultural-semiotic psychology, human development, identity, temporality, and life projects. The formulation presented here is an evolving public synthesis; precise claims about original theoretical contributions require comparison with the doctoral corpus and related manuscripts.

## Connected questions

1. How do social relationships and cultural resources participate in the constitution of imaginable futures?
2. Under which circumstances do people interpret, sustain, revise, or abandon possibilities for action?
3. How do institutional arrangements and artificial interlocutors enter the processes through which judgments and projects are constructed?
4. Which research designs can relate observable variation to the situated meanings and histories relevant to those phenomena?

These questions are interconnected, and none is presented as an independently completed empirical study.

## Four analytical lenses

- **Development, imagination, and possible futures.** Meaning-making, identity, life projects, and temporal relations.
- **Situated judgment and agency.** Uncertainty, choices, possibilities, and conditions of action.
- **Human–AI mediation.** Interlocution, research boundaries, human interpretive authority, and evaluation of technological mediation.
- **Methods and research systems.** Measurement, longitudinal and multilevel analysis, qualitative interpretation, computational experimentation, and provenance.

### Epistemological commitments

Researchers must specify the phenomenon, analytical unit, relevant timescales, sources of evidence, and inferential status before selecting a technique. Statistical patterns, interview accounts, simulations, conceptual propositions, and experimental results support different claims. Cultural-semiotic concepts should contribute to the construction of research problems and interpretation, with their applicability examined in each study.
