# Research Methods & Interpretive Laboratory

The laboratory documents scientific decision-making through reproducible demonstrations and critical interpretation. Its aim is to show how a research question, theoretical perspective, design, measurement strategy, analysis and inference depend on one another.

## Standard case structure

1. **Phenomenon and problem.** Identify what is to be understood or estimated and why the question matters.
2. **Research question and alternatives.** Clarify plausible competing explanations and analytical choices.
3. **Design and measurement.** Specify units, nesting, sampling, repeated observations, construct operationalization and limitations.
4. **Analysis.** Document code, model diagnostics, uncertainty, missing data, sensitivity and robustness where pertinent.
5. **Interpretation.** Relate observable patterns to the problem and distinguish association, causal inference and constitutive explanation.
6. **Criticism and revision.** State alternative interpretations and what further evidence would be needed.

The structure is a documentation aid, not a universal linear workflow.

## First demonstration

[Longitudinal multilevel models with synthetic student trajectories](../05-reproducible-examples/longitudinal-multilevel/README.md)

A synthetic dataset enables examination of time-varying observations within students and students within programmes. The laboratory treats cultural-semiotic interpretation as a question of research design and inference, not a decorative paragraph after statistical results. Simulated observations cannot constitute empirical evidence about actual students.
